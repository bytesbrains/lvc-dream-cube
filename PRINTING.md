# Printing & assembly guide

## 1. Check your cube

The chips are **12 × 12 mm**. That fits a standard 57 mm 3×3 cube, whose stickers or tiles are about 15–16 mm across. If your cube is smaller or bigger, scale the model in your slicer. Scale X and Y only and keep Z (height) at 100%, so the textures stay just as easy to feel.

## 2. Choose your files

| Option | File(s) | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| One cube, one filament | `models/rubik_all54_tiles.3mf` | Fastest route. Chips come out one colour, so paint them or rely on the cube's own colours showing around them. |
| Colour-matched chips | The six `models/rubik_36x_*.3mf` plates | Print each plate in its matching filament. Each plate holds 36 chips, enough for 4 cubes. |

Tactile key: white = smooth · green = frame · orange = ridges · red = rings · blue = dots · yellow = dome.

## 3. Suggested slicer settings

These are starting points, not hard rules. Any FDM printer should work.

- **Material:** PLA or PETG
- **Nozzle:** 0.4 mm
- **Layer height:** 0.12–0.16 mm for dots, rings and dome (crisper detail); 0.2 mm is fine for smooth, frame and ridges
- **Infill:** 100% (the chips are tiny)
- **Supports:** none; every chip prints flat side down
- **First layer:** a clean, flat bed; a textured PEI plate gives the chip backs grip for glue

## 4. Assemble

1. Clean the cube faces with a little isopropyl alcohol.
2. Glue one face at a time, matching each chip to its colour. A thin cyanoacrylate gel or strong double-sided tape both work; use as little as possible so nothing squeezes into the cube's gaps.
3. Keep chips centred on each tile, so they don't catch when the layers turn.
4. Let it cure, then close your eyes and check every face by touch before you scramble it.

## 5. Safety

Chips are small parts. Adults should assemble the cube, and it isn't suitable for children under 3. Check regularly that no chip has come loose.
